Project Overview

Air pollution is one of the most pressing environmental challenges in contemporary India. To understand this complex issue, I designed and built an interactive Air Quality Dashboard using the Frappe Insights platform. The dashboard transforms raw environmental measurements into an intuitive, visual narrative, allowing everyday users and researchers alike to discover, explore, and compare air quality patterns across Indian cities and states.

About the Dataset

The foundation of any good analysis is reliable data. For this project, I used an official, comprehensive dataset sourced from data.gov.in, the government of India's open data platform.

Using a verified government dataset ensured that the insights generated would be grounded in reality and trustworthy. This raw dataset contained detailed parameters including:

  • Comprehensive city and state-level geographic labeling.
  • Specific timestamps and dates for each sampling interval.
  • Individual air quality and Air Quality Index (AQI) metric evaluations.
  • Specific tracking of various particulate matter and gaseous pollutant averages.

Dashboard Design & User Experience

My main goal was to move away from overly complex, intimidating spreadsheets and instead create a tool that was highly engaging, visual, and simple for anyone to interact with. By structuring the dashboard in a natural visual hierarchy, users can immediately get a high-level summary before diving deeper into specific regions or data points.

Analyzing the Core Components:

  • 1. High-Level Summary Metrics (Cards) Placed prominently at the very top, these indicator cards are designed for instant, glanceable insight. The Station Count card gives users an immediate sense of the data scale. Beside it, the Average Pollutant indicator highlights the overall scale of environmental concern.
  • 2. City Comparison (Bar Chart) To compare and rank the diverse locations, I designed a vertical Bar Chart. Bar charts are incredibly effective for this type of categorization, as the human eye can instantly compare the lengths to see which cities suffer from the highest and lowest AQI levels.
  • 3. State-wise Monitoring Footprint (Pie Chart) I integrated a Pie Chart tracking State-wise Station Count, helping users recognize the density of tracking efforts. Visualizing the proportion of stations across states helps demonstrate where focus and infrastructure on environmental monitoring are heaviest.
  • 4. Detailed Proportions (Data Table) At the base, I embedded a Pollutant Proportion and Average Table. This allows users to drill down, verify numbers, and locate high-density pollution records by hand, finding specific peaks that might otherwise get hidden within aggregate averages.

Interactive Explorations and User Control

An interactive dashboard must empower the user to find their own answers. To achieve this, I built and configured two essential interactive Filters:

  • Pollutant Type: Allows users to hone in on specific gases and particles (like PM2.5, PM10, or NO2).
  • State/Geographic Filter: Lets users isolate only the cities within a particular State of interest.

Ultimately, this project highlights how open data and intuitive dashboard tools like Frappe Insights can be bridged to create public tools. By translating complicated rows and columns of environmental statistics into a readable story, we give citizens the visibility they need to understand the invisible air around them.
